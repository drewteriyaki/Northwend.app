Practice money

Feel the ups and downs before using real money

Pick a mix and a monthly amount, choose a date in the past, and see what it would have done on real prices since - the good months and the bad ones. Nothing real is bought, and nothing can be lost. Each month there's also a practice challenge: you pick your own rule, and it's counted only on sticking to it.

  • Practice money

The quiet cost

What a fund's yearly fee costs over time

Every fund takes a small yearly fee, its expense ratio. It looks tiny, but it's taken every year from everything you have. Northwend's fee check shows what yours cost in dollars.

Picking funds in a workplace 401(k)? Decode your plan's fund menu to see what kind of fund each one is and what it charges - free, no account, nothing saved. Inside the app you can also paste a fund's fact sheet and have it explained in plain words.

Choosing a brokerage

Choosing a brokerage, without the sales pitch

A brokerage holds your account - where you buy funds and see what you own. When you're ready, Northwend shows what to compare and some well-known brokerages in alphabetical order. We're not paid by any of them, and we don't rank them.

  • {{CHECK}}Fees. Any yearly account fee, and what buying or selling costs.

  • {{CHECK}}Fractional shares. Buying in dollar amounts, like $50 of a fund.
  • {{CHECK}}Account types. A Roth IRA for retirement, a regular account for anything else.
  • {{CHECK}}An easy app. One you'll be comfortable opening every month.

Once you've started

Help that keeps going after your first investment

Short, optional tools for the months and years after you start. None of them tell you what to buy or sell, and what you write or tick stays in your own account.

  • Words explained

    "What does this mean?" sits beside the harder words in the app, and Learn has every one from A to Z.

  • The monthly walk

    A few minutes once a month: update your holdings, compare your mix with the target you chose, and read one short piece from Learn.

  • Ready for a big drop

    See how your mix held up in 2008, 2020 and 2022, then write down what you'll do if markets fall a lot. If they do, Home shows you your own words.

  • Four times a year

    A short guide on Home when it comes up: contribution limits in January, tax forms in April, workplace benefits in the fall, and your year in review in December.

  • Life changes and lost money

    Life-change guides say what changes, what to gather and whom to ask, for events like a new job or a new baby. Lost & Found lists the official places to look for an old 401(k) or unclaimed money.

  • Explain it to someone

    A private link that shows a partner or family member your plan in plain words - percentages only, never an amount - for 7 or 30 days.

Before you start

Common questions

Do I need money to start?

No. Learning and practice money cost nothing and use pretend money. When you're ready to invest for real, many brokerages let you start small - check each one's own site for its minimums.

Do I need a brokerage account first?

No. The Start investing steps walk you through choosing one and opening it, when you're ready. If you already have one, you can skip straight to bringing it in.

Will Northwend tell me what to buy?

No. It explains what different kinds of investments are and shows examples to learn from, but the choice is always yours. Northwend is an educational tool, not financial advice.

Does Northwend hold my money?

No. Your money stays at your brokerage. Northwend never asks for your brokerage login and can't buy or sell anything for you.

What does it cost?

Northwend is free while it's in beta. It doesn't sell investments, takes no commissions, and no brokerage or fund company pays to be mentioned. If it ever charges for anything, it will say so first.
